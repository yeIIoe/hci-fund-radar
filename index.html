2-year sovereign yields

The single input the FUND is built from. Every card shows how old its reading is.

Why the 2-year yield is the number that matters, and what moves it

It is a forecast, not a price. The 2-year government yield is what the market believes the central bank's policy rate will average over the next two years. It moves before the central bank does, because it is a bet on what the bank will be forced to do.

What pushes it up. Inflation coming in above expectations, a strong labour market, a hawkish tone from the central bank, heavy government issuance, or a global risk-on move out of bonds. Any of these makes the market price more or later cuts.

What pushes it down. Inflation below expectations, rising unemployment, a dovish turn, a flight to safety, or a growth scare — the market prices earlier or deeper cuts.

Why it drives the currency. Capital chases relative return. What matters is never one country's yield on its own, but the spread between two — and, more precisely, the change in that spread. That change is exactly what the FUND measures.

Read the size, not the sign. A 2 bp move is noise; the daily standard deviation of most of these curves sits between 3 and 5 bp. Each card shows the move in standard deviations, so you can tell a real repricing from a rounding.

The honest limit. These are official curves published on each institution's own schedule — from D+1 for the US to weekly for Australia. There is no live 2-year yield here, and there cannot be from these sources. The staleness badge on each card is the truth about that.

What the Pre-FUND is, and how to read it

It flags the indicator, not the price. Each morning it names the pairs whose FUND is most likely to cross into the BEAR band on the next session. That is all it claims.

Why it is useful anyway. The breakout and its zone usually form before the FUND confirms. By the time a pair shows up as tradable, the entry has often already gone. This list buys you the days needed to mark the zone first.

What it is not. Not a trade signal, not a direction, not permission to enter. When rank 1 does flip, the price falls the next day only about as often as a coin toss — that number is in the table below, and it is the reason the zone still has to confirm on its own.

Real cost of holding a position

What each pair charges to be held — per side, measured, not from a brochure.

Why spread alone misleads, and what changes for swing

Raw spread decides nothing. What decides is how much of the available move it eats. A pair with a 2-pip spread that travels 100 pips is cheaper than one with a 1-pip spread that travels 20. A tight spread on a still instrument is a trap.

For swing the swap comes in, and it is asymmetric. Holding 5 trading days costs 7 nights — Wednesday charges 3x to cover the weekend. Because swap follows the rate differential, long and short in the same pair cost differently. That does not exist intraday.

Where the numbers come from. Spread is measured from 1-minute bid x ask (Dukascopy). Swap is estimated from the 2-year differential this radar already ingests, via the carry identity: price x (r_base - r_quote) / 365 / pip. The estimate was calibrated against the broker's own table across 28 pairs and matched at r=0.975 — which is why it can be recomputed here daily without scraping the broker.

The small-differential rule. When |differential| falls below 0.8% there is not enough carry to offset the broker's markup, and the pair is expensive on both sides. Measured: below 0.8% the better side costs 4.29% of the 5-day range; above 2.5%, 0.10%.

Careful with what cost suggests. The cheap side tends to be the carry side — long the high-yielder. Letting cost pick direction is putting on carry without having decided to. Cost breaks ties between theses and sizes them; it never originates one.

Validity. A typical pair flips its cheap side every 8.9 months. This table reflects the current regime, not a permanent truth.

Equities — Research Agent

Different house, different method. No FUND here, no currency pairs.

Why equities and FX are separate systems here

FUND decides FX macro. It compares relative fundamental strength between two currencies from the 2-year rate differential. The unit is the CURRENCY; the pair is derived. None of that applies to a stock.

The Research Agent covers equities. The unit is the COMPANY: target price from a pre-registered multiple times projected earnings, thesis, what could break it, and the market regime. It regenerates a report only when the data behind it really moves — price 3%, earnings projection 10%, results released, regime flip, or 5 sessions.

Why not mix them. Different horizons, data and invalidation criteria. A currency thesis dies when the rate differential turns; a stock thesis dies when projected earnings or the market multiple change. Merged into one score, you could not tell which of the two broke.

What this is NOT. Not an order, not an entry trigger. Fundamental reading with the data that supports it shown alongside.

Commitments of Traders

What COT adds here, and what it does not

Why positioning matters here. FUND measures the reason (rate differentials). COT measures the crowd. Research behind this project found that since 2021 currencies track risk appetite and positioning at least as closely as policy rates — and that the August 2024 unwind was driven by crowded positioning, not by the macro surprise itself.
Status: display only. COT does not touch the FUND score. It is being measured separately before it is allowed to influence any decision.

Why the release itself is not the trade

Trade the repricing, not the print. Price adjusts to a surprise within minutes, so reacting to the release itself is late. What lasts days to weeks is the change in the expected rate path — that is what the FUND captures.
